## What I did myself [confirm]

- Chose the concept (an arcade journey through Homer's *Odyssey*), the eight trials and the direction for the AI rival.
- Set the requirements given to the coding assistants, reviewed their output, played every change, and decided what to keep, change or remove.
- Planned and ran every usability session in person, recorded all task outcomes, times and questionnaire answers, and wrote down participants' quotes.
- Decided which issues to fix after testing, and checked each fix in the game.
- Presented the work.

## What AI tools did not do

- No participant, task result, time, SUS answer, quote or finding was generated by an AI tool. Every number in the test report is calculated from the session records in `Farshore-Phase-3-Test-Data.xlsx`.
- AI tools did not make the final design decisions. Suggestions were accepted only after review.

## How AI output was checked

- Code: the automated test suite (`npm test`), the TypeScript build (`npm run build`), and manual play of each affected screen.
- Documents: read and edited by me against the game and the raw data. Calculations in the report were cross-checked against the workbook formulas.

## AI inside the product (part of the design, not tool use)

- **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** (`qwen3:4b-instruct`, local Ollama, or OpenAI/Groq/OpenRouter for signed-in players): chooses each rival's strategy and opening taunt before a round, and writes live bard and rival commentary. The game code owns scoring, collisions and progress.
- **Kokoro-82M**: speaks the commentary. The browser's own voice is the fallback.
- Local algorithms (A* pathfinding and scored decision rules) move the rivals during play. These are authored code, not trained models.
