1. Screen designs

High-fidelity screens of the tested prototype, captured from the running game and annotated. The same frames are in the Figma file (link to be added). Numbered markers match the notes beside each screen: gold = interface, cyan = AI behavior or AI state. Failure states were produced with the mock AI service (Section 1.2), and the interface shown is the real one.

1.2 AI components: five states and user control

The main AI feature is the rival's strategy. Before each round, the model reads the player's recent attempts and chooses how the mythic rival will play. It appears in the briefing's rival panel (flow F3) and is designed in all five states. The explainable AI Insights panel in Cyclops' Cave has its own empty state (D23).

StateWhat the player seesFrames
1. Empty / initialThe briefing opens before any strategy exists. Preparation starts at once, so the panel opens in the loading design at 0 s rather than as an empty box. With no model connected, the initial state is "No AI rival connected", with three ways to connect and Check again. In Cyclops' Cave, AI Insights reads "Observing. No committed action." until the rival's first decision.D06, D10, D23
2. Loading"[Rival] is studying you", an elapsed-seconds counter and a cyan scanning bar, which does not pretend to know the remaining time. Matched to measured latency: the Phase 2 spike measured 7.3-11.6 s (mean 8.3 s) per strategy, and a CPU laptop with Qwen3-4B takes about 15 s. The mock's slow mode (12 s) tests this. Cancel, or Keep the previous strategy if there is one.D06, D25
3. SuccessThe strategy name, "Strategy chosen by [model]", a plain description, the rival's taunt (with Hear it), and Ask for another strategy. Start is enabled.D07, D13
4. Low confidence"The rival's plan was set aside". The model's strategy did not pass the game's checks, so it was not used and nothing changed. The main action is Ask for another strategy. Keep the previous strategy appears if one exists. Amber, not red: nothing is broken.D22
5. Error, with recovery"The rival is not ready", with a plain message and no codes. One clear action, Prepare the rival again, plus "Nothing about your voyage changes". Keep the previous strategy appears if one exists. Task 4 of the test leads participants here.D09

User control over AI results

ControlHow it worksFrames
Regenerate"Ask for another strategy" on a ready strategy, and as the main action in the low-confidence state. The model chooses again.D07, D24
UndoAfter asking again, "Undo: back to [previous strategy]" restores it, and pressing Undo again swaps back. While a new strategy is loading, or if it fails or is set aside, "Keep the previous strategy" returns to the old one.D24, D25, D22
RejectThe game rejects a strategy that fails its checks before it can be used (low-confidence state). Players can reject the AI commentary by switching commentary or voice off in Settings, or with the Voice button on the menu.D22, D18
Cancel"Cancel preparation" stops a request in progress. The pilot found that the briefing then starts a new request at once. This is noted for the sessions.D06
EditNot offered by design: the strategy is the opponent's move. Changing the difficulty asks the model for a new strategy at the new level.D08

Mock AI service

All AI calls (rival status, strategy and live commentary) go through one module, server/lib/model-api.ts. It asks server/lib/mock-ai.ts first. The mock answers in the modes success, slow, uncertain, error and offline, plus "-once" modes that fail once and then recover. A moderator switches modes from a second browser tab at /api/ai-mock?mode=..., or for the whole server with FARSHORE_AI_MOCK. Farshore's live model is already connected (change A2), so in Phase 4 the mock stays as the test fixture for these states instead of being replaced. Frames marked "mock" were captured in the real interface with the mock answering, and name the model as "mock-ai (test mode)".

The instructor's Usability Test Script Template, filled in for Farshore (Farshore-Usability-Test-Script.docx). The same script is used for every participant, and Phase 5 repeats it for the before-and-after comparison.

Session information

Setup before each session (about 3 minutes)

  1. Open the Ollama app and run npm.cmd run dev. Open http://localhost:3000, wait for AI rival online, and open one trial briefing yourself so the model is warm.
  2. Open a new InPrivate (Edge) or Incognito (Chrome) window at http://localhost:3000, so each participant starts a fresh game. Press F11.
  3. Open session-logger.html on a second screen or device, or use a printed copy of the script. Enter the participant ID.
  4. Have the four task cards and the SUS form ready.

1. Welcome and consent (read aloud)

"Thank you for helping us. We are students in an HCI course testing an app we built that uses AI. We are testing the app, not you. If something is confusing, that is a problem with our design, and that is exactly what we need to find.

I will ask you to do a few tasks. Please think out loud as you go: say what you are looking for, what you expect to happen, and anything that surprises you. I will not be able to help you during the tasks, because we need to see what happens without us.

This will take about 20 minutes. Your name will not appear in our report. You can stop at any time, and you can skip any task, with no explanation needed. Please do not enter any personal or sensitive information into the app.

Do you have any questions before we start?"

Record: consent given (yes/no). Sessions are not recorded unless the participant agrees.

2. Warm-up questions (2 minutes)

  1. How often do you play video games, and what kind?
  2. When you play against a computer opponent, how do you work out what it is planning?
  3. Have you used AI tools (such as ChatGPT) or played games with AI-driven characters before?

3. Tasks

Read each task aloud and hand over the printed card. Start timing when they begin. Stop when they say they are done or give up. Record: completed (Yes / Partly / No / Gave up), time on task, errors or wrong turns, and quotes. After Tasks 1-3, ask: "How easy or difficult was that, from 1 (very difficult) to 5 (very easy)? Why?"

Task 1, from primary flow F1 (start or resume and learn a trial)

  • Task wording: "You've heard about a game where you travel home with Odysseus, and you've just opened it for the first time. Start a new journey and get ready for the first challenge."
  • Success criterion: the Land of the Lotus-Eaters briefing is on screen (it shows "YOUR OBJECTIVE").

Task 2, from primary flow F2 (prepare, review and accept a model strategy)

  • Task wording: "Before you face your first opponent, you want to know what you're up against. Find out what your opponent plans to do in this challenge, and who or what decided that plan."
  • Success criterion: they paraphrase the strategy in the rival panel and say the AI model chose it ("Strategy chosen by qwen3:4b-instruct").

Task 3, from primary flow F3 (play against an active mythic rival)

  • Moderator setup (untimed): Settings > Unlock all islands, close Settings, open the Voyage map. Say: "I've unlocked another island to save time."
  • Task wording: "A friend told you the Cyclops' Cave is the toughest island, and that you can see how the Cyclops thinks. Play it, and while you play, tell me why the Cyclops made one of his moves, using what the game shows you."
  • Success criterion: they cite a reason or action from the AI Insights (or AI Lab) panel for a specific throw.
  • Also record: the game result, and whether they noticed the AI Insights panel without prompting.

Task 4, the AI failure task, from primary flow F4 (recover from a failed or unsuitable AI plan)

  • Moderator setup: in a second tab of the same browser window, open http://localhost:3000/api/ai-mock?mode=error-once, then close that tab. Do not warn the participant.
  • Task wording: "You'd like to try a different island. Open the Island of Aeolus and get ready to play it."
  • What the AI does: an error. The mock AI service fails the first strategy request, so the briefing shows "The rival is not ready" with "Prepare the rival again". The retry succeeds.
  • Record: did they notice something was wrong (yes/no)? What did they do next? Did they recover without help (yes/no)? Success means they reach a ready rival (the start button reads "...counts toward your voyage") without help.
  • After the session, open http://localhost:3000/api/ai-mock?mode=off.

4. Moderator rules

If they are completely stuck and distressed, you may end the task. Record it as not completed and note where they stopped.

5. System Usability Scale (after all tasks)

The participant rates each statement from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Do not explain or reword the statements. If asked, say "whatever it means to you."

  1. I think that I would like to use this app frequently.
  2. I found the app unnecessarily complex.
  3. I thought the app was easy to use.
  4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this app.
  5. I found the various functions in this app were well integrated.
  6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this app.
  7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this app very quickly.
  8. I found the app very awkward to use.
  9. I felt very confident using the app.
  10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this app.

Scoring: for odd-numbered items subtract 1 from the score; for even-numbered items subtract the score from 5. Add the ten adjusted values and multiply by 2.5. The result is 0-100, and about 68 is average.

6. AI trust and perception questions

  1. On a scale of 1 (not at all) to 5 (completely), how much did you trust the results the app gave you? Why?
  2. How did you know whether a result was good or not?
  3. When the app had trouble, what did you think had happened?
  4. Was there anything you wanted to change about a result but could not?
  5. Would you use this instead of how you do it today? Why or why not?

7. Debrief

"That's everything. Two last questions: what was the most frustrating moment, and what worked best? Is there anything you expected the app to do that it didn't?"

Thank them. Do not explain what they "should" have done, and do not justify the design.

After the session

  1. Within 10 minutes, write your top 3 observations while they are fresh.
  2. In the logger, click Export JSON and save it as phase3/data/P#.json. This fills the results spreadsheet: completion, time on task, ease, SUS score and trust rating.
  3. Do not change the script between participants. Note any change you were forced to make, and why.
  4. Keep the completed script. Phase 5 reuses the same tasks and measures.

Analysis plan

  • Per task: success rate (Yes), completion including Partly, median completed time, mean errors, and mean ease (1-5).
  • SUS per participant, plus mean and standard deviation, compared with the 68 average. AI trust: mean, median and share rating 4 or 5, with the reasons.
  • Task 4: share who noticed the failure and share who recovered without help.
  • Issues are rated 0 (not a problem) to 4 (usability catastrophe) by frequency, impact and persistence. The most severe are fixed before the midterm and shown before and after. The rest are listed for Phase 4.

Materials used with this script: task cards, the SUS and AI trust questionnaire, the observer sheet and the session logger (phase3/kit/).

3. Test report

3.1 Method

Moderated, in-person think-aloud sessions with five or six adults who had not seen Farshore, run by the author with the script in Section 2 (the instructor's template, filled in). Each session took about 20 minutes and had four tasks (T1-T4), on a Windows laptop running the prototype locally. Tasks 1-3 used Qwen3-4B-Instruct through Ollama. Task 4 used the mock AI service to trigger an AI error without warning. Measures, as on the template: completion (Yes / Partly / No / Gave up), time on task, errors or wrong turns, ease from 1 to 5 after Tasks 1-3, and for Task 4 whether the participant noticed the failure and recovered without help. After all tasks: the System Usability Scale, and the template's five AI trust and perception questions (trust in the results, 1-5, with the reason).

Analysis: success rate per task (Yes only, and including Partly), median time on task, mean ease, SUS scored by the standard method and compared with the 68 average, mean AI trust, and an issue list rated on the 0-4 severity scale (0 = not a problem, 4 = usability catastrophe). All calculations are live formulas in the raw data workbook. These results are the baseline that Phase 5 repeats.

Things to watch for during sessions (pilot observations, to be confirmed or not by participants)

  1. The start button is below the fold. At 1440 × 900 the briefing's "Face [rival]" button sits below the visible area, so players must scroll past the objective and the rival panel to start (T1, T2).
  2. The Lotus strategy has no gameplay effect. In the Lotus-Eaters maze, the guardians' behavior depends only on difficulty. The strategy the model chooses (Garden patrol / Maze pursuit / Junction interception) is shown, but the maze code never reads it. This matters for T2 and AT1 to AT3 (AI honesty). Recommendation: either make the plan change guardian behavior or say so in the panel. Decision (2026-11-04): tested as-is. Watch whether participants notice in T2 and T3. It will be fixed after the sessions and reported as a change.
  3. The plan name repeats and the panel disagrees with the HUD. The rival panel title "Maze pursuit" is repeated as the first words of its description. In play, the HUD says "Maze pursuit" under the rival but "Patrol · guardians follow the garden paths" as the live line. Players may read these as conflicting (T2).
  4. The Lotus side panel is clipped. At 1440 × 900 the "Bonus challenge" card in the encounter side panel is cut off at the bottom.
  5. Medium is the default difficulty. New players may not notice the selector (not a task in the 20-minute protocol).
  6. On a phone, AI Insights sit below the play area. In Cyclops' Cave at 390 × 844 the Insights panel is under the game and touch controls, so it cannot be read while dodging. Sessions run on a laptop, so this is a design note rather than a test task.
  7. On a phone, the briefing is long. The rival's strategy and the start button are several screens below the objective.
  8. The voyage chart labels overlap. Some island labels overlap the "wine-dark sea" caption at 1440 × 900.
  9. Model availability can flicker under load. Once during the automated run, the status check briefly reported "AI rival offline" while the laptop was busy, then recovered by itself. If it happens in a session, note it. It may affect T2 waiting times.
  10. "Cancel preparation" starts again straight away. Cancelling returns the panel to its initial state, and the briefing then starts a new preparation by itself, so cancelling only restarts the wait. Watch whether anyone tries it in T2.
  11. The start button says "The model is choosing a strategy" when nothing is being prepared. In the error and low-confidence states the disabled start button keeps that label, which can mislead (T4).
