# Farshore: story sources for the presentation

Checked September 26, 2026.

## Text you can put on a slide

**Narrative source:** Homer’s *Odyssey*. Farshore adapts the return of Odysseus from Troy to Ithaca, including the wanderings, the crisis in his household, and the final recognition and peace. The game follows the journey chronologically, while Homer begins near the end and recounts earlier adventures through Odysseus’s story to the Phaeacians. Player advice, trust scores, month costs, and alternative routes are original game mechanics.

**Short citation:** Homer, *Odyssey*, trans. Samuel Butler (1900), Books 1–24, Project Gutenberg, eBook 1727.

[Read the complete primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1727/1727-h/1727-h.htm).

This is a source for the literary story. It does not establish that the hero, gods, monsters, or every event existed in documented history. The game uses the Greek names Odysseus, Athena, Poseidon, and Zeus. Butler often uses Ulysses, Minerva, Neptune, and Jove for the same figures.

## Explain the game’s departures clearly

- The player is an imagined counsellor. Homer does not include this player character or the game’s dialogue.
- The ten years of war and long absence provide the frame. The exact month costs and the objective of finishing before twenty total years are game rules, not a reconstruction of every sailing day in the poem.
- Calypso’s seven years and Circe’s year are stated in the source notes. The playable version permits shorter stays.
- A sealed wind bag can carry the fleet straight to Ithaca. This deliberately changes Homer’s outcome and skips the later wandering chapters.
- Sparing the cattle can preserve the remaining crew and avoid the wreck and Calypso’s island. The game now follows that consequence instead of inexplicably making Odysseus alone in the next chapter.
- Escapes from the Sirens after a mistake, attempted rescue at the Laestrygonian harbour, and recovery from revealing himself early are authored alternatives.
- The full route has fifteen playable chapters. Some events are compressed into chapter context or consequences. The bow contest and battle are condensed within the hidden-king chapter; the recognition and peace have their own final chapter.
- The AI interprets counsel within an authored scene. It does not supply the literary evidence. The primary text and the fixed source notes supply that evidence.

## Map source and limits

The coastline layer uses **Natural Earth, 1:50m Land**, clipped to the Mediterranean region and simplified for the game’s SVG chart.

- [Dataset description and download](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-physical-vectors/50m-land/)
- [Natural Earth terms of use: public-domain data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)
- [Exact upstream GeoJSON used](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_50m_land.geojson)

Modern coastlines provide geographic context. They are not a reconstruction of Bronze Age shorelines. Troy and modern Ithaki are geographic anchors; Ismarus is indicated regionally in Thrace. The Cyclopes, Aeolia, Aeaea, Ogygia, Scheria, and other legendary locations use illustrative placements. Oceanus is a symbolic position at the edge of the chart. The connecting lines show narrative sequence, not verified navigation tracks.

**Map slide caption:** “Mediterranean coastlines: Natural Earth, public domain. Odyssey route and legendary locations: illustrative interpretation based on Homer’s narrative; not an established historical itinerary.”
